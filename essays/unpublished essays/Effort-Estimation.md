# IDPM Effort Estimation and Tracking

## Estimating Estimates

### How did I make these estimates?
In Starting this final project, I aimed to showcase my ability to not only learn new concepts, but to absorb and ultimately apply them. A large part of being able to display that was timing and effort. My initial thoughts were confident, and I felt truly able to add valuable contributions to the group. Surprisingly, this task was more difficult than I expected. What I've come to understand of this, is that as a beginner, inaccurate estimates are expected and improve the more seasoned one becomes in practice.

Upon making rough estimates for how long each task would take, I had to understand exactly what each one demanded of me. This was simply the roughest part of participating in the configuration of the web application. Based on various assignments throughout the semester, I tried to take a very realistic approach to how long I thought tasks would take. I rounded up past practice WODs and in class WODs to give my best guesses for a singular task. Most, if not all of the estimates I made were very far off from the reality of carrying them out. I largely attritbute this to the fact that many of the fundamental parts of the site likely involved sub-tasks, which of course, affected the overall duration.

### Was estimating in advance beneficial?
What I found within these estimates was that they were not necessarily directly advantageous to the project itself, but to my own personal time management skills. Understanding the need for more or less time to complete a task was extremely important to team cohesion, testing, and class deadlines. I took these estimates as an opportunity to improve within each given milestone. So, I guess I can say estimating my times in advance was helpful for myself and my team's personal gain. 

## Effort Tracking

### Was tracking effort useful?
Tracking my efforts proved more challenging in terms of organization and precision. Despite this challenge, I found tracking my efforts between coding and non-coding efforts was very helpful to the acknowledgement of my own progress. That way, I was able to see where I was falling short and where I was making better efforts. I often found that I was typically careless in tracking my efforts, forgetting to split up the duration of different efforts, i.e. differentiating research into further understanding of Prisma, or how to implement a function versus writing said function in a file. 

### What tools were used to track efforts?
In order to semi-accurately track all of my efforts, I used my easily accessible apple timer, available on both my phone and laptop. I thought it best to use it because it didn't require much thought and was convenient. I exclusively relied on github commits and visual studio code to depict my coding efforts, starting from the opening of a file or branch to several commits in between to try and separate my coding from my non-coding efforts. 

## Reflection

### What would I do different next time?
If I had to redo this project, start to finish, I would do a few things differently. First off, I would be a little more intentional about making estimates without being prideful. Transparently, I underestimated tasks because I'd already considered my level of development lower than the other members of my team. Unfortunately, this only showed me that I needed to catch up, instead of meeting myself where I am and allowing myself to work through the novelty of some concepts. I would also track my effort differently, as towards the end of the last milestone, I found it very helpful to commit changes before doing other non-coding based research. This way, efforts would be tracked singularly and not overlapping with each other.

### AI Use
When absolutely stuck on small implementations, I sought to use Gemini or Copilot. I largely used these tools to aid in Prisma disparities, and in correspondence with one another to "cross reference" ideas. Unsurprisingly, I ran into a lot of issues with Prisma and spent a lot of time with AI resources trying to figure out why. In all honestly, this was the bulk of AI utilization for this project, with some small coding errors here and there. For example, I typically prompted one of the above mentioned tools to "help me understand why" seeding our database kept failing. I gave it context of what has already been tried e.g. different imports added, updated configuration files, updated prisma file, before prompting it for a solution. Throughout this process, there were a lot of trial and errors as AI is not foolproof. It should not act as the sole "brain", especially in a project that details socially responsible computing. In receving responses or outputs from any of these tools, there were either deletions, revisions, or only small parts used in the final draft of code. This is because there were many solutions given that didn't quite fit the problem. 
All in all, there were mistakes made on my end, but I have learned what types of things I can do in the future to not only be more efficient in coding, but in being more intentional about understanding, accurately documenting, and improving my efforts.
